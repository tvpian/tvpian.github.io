What infrastructure is required to turn human demonstrations, interventions, and failures into policies that can be evaluated and safely returned to a physical robot?

System architecture

One evidence path, not disconnected demos.

The design treats each rollout as evidence that must survive a traceable sequence: human operation, synchronized capture, dataset construction, model versioning, evaluation, and explicit authorization. A failure is not discarded; it can become a corrective segment for the next training cycle.

Development stages

Built from the robot outward.

The platform was not assembled around a single model. It was developed around repeatable system boundaries so policies, controllers, sensors, and evaluation methods can change without rebuilding the complete cell.

  1. 01

    Define stable interfaces

    Common observation, action, episode, and deployment interfaces separate learned-policy development from robot-specific and simulator-specific backends.

  2. 02

    Collect synchronized demonstrations

    Teleoperation, multimodal recording, episode review, and conversion are connected so training data retains timing and task context rather than becoming a loose collection of files.

  3. 03

    Mirror critical paths in simulation

    The digital twin is used for control-path validation, policy integration, and controlled transfer studies. It is not presented as a perfect replica of contact or sensing.

  4. 04

    Train and diagnose policies

    Policies move through offline checks, representation diagnostics, state and prompt ablations, simulation, and staged physical tests before broader rollout.

  5. 05

    Turn intervention into data

    Human takeover and corrective motion can be recorded, reviewed, converted, and mixed into later training sets. Automated evaluation remains under development.

My contribution is the architecture that makes experiments comparable, recoverable, and deployable.

I designed and implemented the reusable teleoperation and data-collection infrastructure, synchronization and dataset-conversion path, policy deployment interfaces, simulation integration, staged test workflow, and the mechanism for recording human interventions as corrective data.

The compliance-controller study on a bimanual setup was explored using this infrastructure with an intern I supervised. That extension is described as supervised work rather than presented as an individual contribution.
