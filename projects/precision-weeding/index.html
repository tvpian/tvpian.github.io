Plant–robot interaction · 2021–23

Plan around
living geometry.

How can a manipulator reach weeds when the surrounding plants are deformable, irregular, and unsafe to treat as ordinary rigid obstacles?

This work connects image-derived stem and leaf geometry to a hybrid inverse-kinematics planner and a purpose-built weeding tool. The goal is not merely to detect weeds, but to convert plant structure into a reachable workspace and a lower-contact action sequence.

System architecture

Recover geometry before commanding motion.

The vision pipeline extracts task geometry—not only class labels. That geometry reshapes the robot's reachable space before the planner selects a manipulation sequence.

Development stages

Co-design perception, planning, and contact.

The project treats the end effector and the motion strategy as part of the same safety problem.

  1. 01

    Estimate plant structure

    Thresholding, morphological processing, and shape estimation recover stem locations, leaf spread, and relevant task boundaries from the observed bed.

  2. 02

    Convert irregular layouts into task order

    A flower-carpet-inspired representation organizes the plant bed into an ordered sequence rather than treating every plant independently.

  3. 03

    Warp the reachable workspace

    The planning space is adjusted around delicate geometry before the hybrid IK stage selects a robot configuration.

  4. 04

    Join learned and analytic components

    A feedforward network and inverse-kinematics reasoning contribute complementary parts of the task-planning pipeline.

  5. 05

    Co-design the tool

    The AGGRIP concept combines mechanical plucking with targeted spray or watering actions to support multiple plant-care operations while reducing crop contact.

I worked across the perception-to-action chain and co-designed the task tool.

My work connected plant perception, geometric task representation, hybrid inverse-kinematics planning, and physical manipulation. I also co-designed the AGGRIP end-effector with a mechanical engineer.

The page describes the collaborative research program and preserves the distinction between my system contribution and the mechanical co-design process.

Interpretation boundary. The system demonstrates a plant-aware planning approach. It should not be read as proof of agronomic safety across crop types or long-duration field operation.
