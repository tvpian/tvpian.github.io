PoseFusion + fall detection · 2023

Reasoning from
partial evidence.

Can a model preserve useful spatial and temporal structure when viewpoints change or important joints disappear?

Across two related studies, we represented people as pose graphs, used graph convolution to encode body structure, used transformers to model action through time, and investigated how multiple views could contribute complementary evidence under occlusion.

Architecture

Preserve structure before fusing views.

The central design choice was to avoid flattening raw coordinates directly into a temporal model. Each frame first retains skeletal topology, each view produces its own embedding, and only then are the view-specific representations combined and passed through time.

Experimental program

Test what disappears.

The experiments isolate changes in viewpoint, occluded body regions, temporal sampling, model architecture, aggregation method, and embedding size.

  1. 01

    Transformer versus GCN + Transformer

    Tests whether explicit spatial relationships between joints add value before temporal encoding.

  2. 02

    Single-view versus multi-view

    Measures whether unoccluded views can compensate for missing or ambiguous evidence elsewhere.

  3. 03

    Occlude structured body regions

    Lower body, torso, and upper-limb masks expose which missing joints most strongly affect fall recognition.

  4. 04

    Change camera view

    Training on the front view and testing on alternative views probes cross-view generalization.

  5. 05

    Skip frames

    Increasing temporal stride tests how much motion information can be removed before recognition degrades.

I designed the multimodal sequential architecture used across the two studies.

My core contribution was the model architecture connecting graph-based spatial encoding, temporal transformers, and view-specific aggregation, along with the experimental framing used to inspect viewpoint and occlusion behavior.

The studies were collaborative. The page reports the shared research results while distinguishing architectural contribution from sole authorship of the complete papers, code, datasets, or experiments.

Interpretation boundary. These results support robustness within the reported datasets and ablations. They do not establish deployment readiness or general human understanding.
