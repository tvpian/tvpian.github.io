Multimodal UAV · 2024

Track what
a person means.

Can one aerial system accept a click, box, image, or text description, recover the intended target, and close the loop through motion?

Track Anything Rapter (TAR) connects open-vocabulary and one-shot perception to real-time tracking and visual servoing on a PX4-enabled VOXL2 M500 platform. The system was evaluated in simulation and on a physical UAV against Vicon-derived trajectories.

Closed-loop system

Meaning becomes motion through explicit interfaces.

The system separates target specification, initial localization, segmentation and feature extraction, temporal tracking, re-detection, and UAV control. This made it possible to compare input modalities without replacing the full downstream stack.

Development stages

From one-shot perception to a flying system.

Each stage addressed a different failure mode: ambiguous specification, imperfect masks, target loss, delayed perception, and unstable motion.

  1. 01

    Integrate one-shot and open-vocabulary perception

    DINO, CLIP, and SAM were assembled so the desired object could be located from different human query formats without training a closed-set detector for every target.

  2. 02

    Carry the target through time

    Once localized, the target mask and feature representation seed the tracker. Periodic re-detection helps recover identity after drift or partial target loss.

  3. 03

    Bridge workstation and flight controller

    A ROS 2 vision node processes the streamed camera feed, while the control node converts image-space error into filtered velocity and yaw targets for PX4 offboard control.

  4. 04

    Validate before physical flight

    Gazebo, QGroundControl, RTSP streaming, and the control interface were exercised before moving to the VOXL2-enabled physical platform.

  5. 05

    Compare trajectories rather than isolated detections

    Vicon-derived drone and target positions were aligned using Dynamic Time Warping to evaluate the behavior of the complete perception-control loop.

I led the foundation-model perception work and co-integrated it with tracking and control.

I spearheaded implementation of the one-shot detection and segmentation components using DINO, CLIP, and SAM. I then worked with my collaborator on pose extraction, the perception proof of concept, and integration of the one-shot module with the real-time tracker.

The tracking baseline and full system were collaborative work. The page therefore describes TAR as co-developed and does not convert shared implementation into sole authorship.

Interpretation boundary. DTW measures trajectory agreement for the reported runs. It does not independently isolate perception, controller, network, or vehicle dynamics errors.
